Cover story · p. 04

The capability came first. Every time.

Sean A. Russell has spent three decades building the thing before the business existed to hold it. With LESARUSS, he is finally building both at once, and he is doing it with the community.

Feature7 minute readSubject: Sean A. Russell

“The vision was never too big. The box was.”

The LESARUSS tagline

At fourteen, in 1996, Sean A. Russell was already being paid to build websites for nonprofits, for family and for local businesses. A year later, in a 10th grade logo assignment, he named the company he would spend the next three decades growing into: LESARUSS. It launched in 2000 and was incorporated in 2007.

The pattern was set early. From about 1997 to 2002 he founded and ran the Ultimate Championship Federation, an online writing federation with two full shows a week and up to thirty writers under his judging. The TV production magnet at Hallandale High and an NBC Junior Broadcaster opportunity handed him the tools of broadcast. He put them to work immediately.

The media years

Anime 3000 came next, starting around 2003. It grew to about thirty writers, launched podcasts in 2007 and went on to pass one million podcast downloads, with sponsors and advertisers that included Sony, Nintendo, Viz Media and Funimation. It was invited to the first New York Anime Festival, and Crunchyroll asked him to join its first news team.

From 2007 to 2012, Top Spot USA covered independent music: 181 videos, 116 artists, 710,245 views, Warped Tour six or seven times, and the first outlet to interview Hey Monday. It produced its own shows. Along the way he worked with or alongside MTV, ABC, NBC, Nintendo, Crunchyroll, the Miami HEAT, the Miami Dolphins and the Miami Marlins.

I’ve always been a Community Builder, even before having the language for what I was doing.

Sean A. Russell

The classroom underneath it all

Education has run beneath every project since 2003. Russell’s Roving Reporters, from about 2007 to 2019, put elementary students in charge of full video production before the first bell. He served as District Webmaster for Broward County Public Schools from 2014 to 2017 and returned in 2024, where he is today, supporting district communications that included an $800M bond measure voters approved.

In 2017 he took The Leap and went full-time entrepreneur. SoFlo Vegans, about ten years of community and hundreds of interviews, grew into Vegans Explore and its goal of 1,000 communities by 2030. From 2017 to 2023 he chaired marketing for Seed Food and Wine.

The order changes

Every one of those projects proved the capability before a business model existed to hold it. The audience came, the work was real, and the business lagged behind. LESARUSS changes the order.

Sean built its operating system himself, with AI: HQ, the brand sites, an outreach engine, membership and payments. This week it launched three sites in a single day. Since March 2026, GeekFon Society, an animated music universe, has put 121 songs live across a roster of animated artists.

The difference this time is who builds with him. LESARUSS is the first time the business is being built with the community, not after it.
